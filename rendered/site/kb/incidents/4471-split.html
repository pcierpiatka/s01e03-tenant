Ticket #4471 was merged on subject lines, as decision 0001 says to. Its page calls it one issue with one fix queued. The code under that page does not agree, and neither do the report bodies. This page splits the ticket into the problems it really holds, counts each one from a membership table, and files one ticket per problem.

Where this page and the incident page disagree, the code wins. The incident page is a claim written in September. The tables below are computed from the export at render time.

The problems

Every unique report in the export has exactly one row in the membership table: the problem its body describes. The desk counts a report where its cause files it, so the one-day-move reports are counted under the parser as well and footnoted there, the way readAs does it on the incident page. The ticket counts overlap by exactly those reports, and the table says so. The table is built from the membership at render time; the export’s duplicates by id are removed first, the way uniqueById does it.

Parser flips day and month. The shared parser reads every date month first. Tenants in day-first locales enter a day-first date, and it comes back with the two swapped. This is what the incident page describes, and it is the ticket’s own cause.

The booking-v3 hint tells people to type month first. Under its date field, booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. People at tenants on that form type what the hint says, and the form then reads it the tenant’s way. The symptom is the same swap, the fix is a different one, and it lives in the form, not the parser. causes.ts already separates it; the desk never gave it a ticket.

Dates move by one day. A handful of bodies do not describe a swap at all: the invite and the calendar disagree by one day in the same month, two cities see two different days for one meeting, or an invite arrives before the booking was made. The desk noted these as “read as a one-day move” and footnoted them under the parser. A swap cannot produce them, so they get a ticket of their own, and they stay counted under the parser where the desk filed them.

How the reports were placed

  1. One id, one report. The export pages overlap; uniqueById keeps one record per id.
  2. Cause by code. causeOf gives each report its cause from tenant, form and channel.
  3. Blind read. Readers saw each body without its cause or intake tag and recorded the symptom it describes: what was entered, what was shown, expected and actual.
  4. Adjudication. Where the body’s symptom is not a swap, or describes no defect, one adjudicator placed the report and wrote down why. Those reasons are in the membership table.
  5. Second opinion. A fresh reader re-checked every disputed report and a fixed-seed sample of the rest.
  6. One source of numbers. One script checks that every unique report has exactly one row, that the rows total the export’s unique count, that each ticket’s count equals the length of its id list, and that the parser and hint tickets cover exactly the reports causes.ts gives those causes. Nothing on this page or in the tickets is typed by hand.

The membership table, the per-ticket id lists and the script are in the repository linked below.

Where the kb disagrees with itself

  • The incident page says one issue. CauseTable on the same page shows two causes.
  • desk-notes.json lists reports the desk read as a one-day move and keeps them under the parser. The bodies describe a different defect, so this page gives them a ticket of their own, and keeps the desk’s count where it is.
  • Decision 0001 merges by subject. DedupeExplorer shows the same subject with different causes and different subjects with the same cause. The decision is accepted and is not edited here; a new record should replace it.
